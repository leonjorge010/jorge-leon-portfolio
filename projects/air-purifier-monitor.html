Air Purifier Monitor

Overview

A DIY air quality monitor and purifier built on Arduino — sensors, a round touchscreen UI, and automatic fan control when air quality degrades.

The problem

Indoor air quality can change quickly, but most consumer setups either measure or purify — not both in a closed loop. I wanted a compact system that could read the air in real time, show clear feedback on a display, and respond automatically when particulate levels rise.

What I built

The system reads particulate and environmental data, displays it on a round GC9A01A touchscreen, and drives a fan that ramps up when measured air quality gets worse. A PMS5003 handles PM2.5 readings, with temperature and humidity from a DHT22 (and related sensors in the companion PCB build).

Firmware coordinates multiple communication buses on the Arduino — SPI, I2C, and UART — so the display, sensors, and control logic can run together as one sealed unit rather than a loose bench prototype.

Challenges

Fitting sensor reads, UI updates, and fan control into a responsive loop without starving any peripheral took careful timing. Power was another constraint: the display, sensors, and logic drew more than a typical Uno onboard regulator could sustain when everything ran together, which pushed the design toward a custom shield and a dedicated buck converter path in the PCB version of the project.

Current state

The monitor runs as a complete embedded product: live air quality on the touchscreen, automatic purification response, and a codebase published on GitHub. It also became the platform for a custom PCB shield so the build could move from jumper wires into a clean, enclosure-ready assembly.
