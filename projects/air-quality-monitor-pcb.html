Air Quality Monitor PCB

Overview

A custom Arduino Uno shield for an air quality monitor and purifier — sensors, touchscreen, power conversion, and MOSFET fan drive on a compact through-hole board.

The problem

The air quality monitor worked on the bench, but jumper wires were a liability inside a sealed enclosure. I needed a custom shield that could host the sensors, display, and fan drive cleanly — and solve the power limits of running everything off the Uno’s onboard regulator.

What I built

Designed for Professor Paulsen’s Microcontroller System Design class, this shield brings the air quality monitor into a clean, enclosure-ready form. A PMS5003 reads PM2.5, an SGP30 tracks VOCs and eCO₂, an SHT31-D handles temperature and humidity, a round GC9A01A touchscreen shows the UI, and a 12V fan ramps up when air quality degrades.

The layout maximizes the Uno’s available I/O across SPI, I2C, and UART. A single 12V barrel-jack input splits on-board: one branch powers the fan directly, the other feeds an MP1584 buck converter that steps down to 5V for the Arduino. A MOSFET stage drives the fan. The board was designed with through-hole parts from the lab so it could be built without waiting on specialty SMD stock, and sent to JLCPCB for fabrication.

Challenges

The combined current draw of the display, sensors, and logic exceeded the Uno’s onboard regulator, so the power architecture had to change from a simple dual-rail idea to a single 12V input with on-board buck conversion. At 2.7″ × 2.1″, fitting four sensor connectors, the buck converter, the MOSFET driver, and supporting passives took several placement passes — including putting the MP1584 on the underside so it sits between the shield and the Uno.

Current state

The shield replaces loose jumper wires with a reliable interconnect for a sealed enclosure (designed with project partner Manuel Alcaraz). Boards went to JLCPCB with the software already complete, ready for final assembly and testing. The project also applied PCB skills from Dr. Butler’s design class to a full microcontroller system, not just a schematic exercise.
